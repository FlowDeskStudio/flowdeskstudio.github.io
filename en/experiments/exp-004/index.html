EXPERIMENT #004 / COMPLETE

I had an AI copy
a drawing 100 times

The subject came through to the end.
What changed was the details.

2026.10.09 CLAUDE OPUS 5.5 3 × 100 STEPS

The result first.

An AI drew a picture. The next AI saw only that picture and copied it. It is the telephone game, played with drawings. This was repeated 100 times for each of three subjects.

The round-100 pictures were shown to a separate AI that knew nothing about the experiment. For all three, it named the original subject correctly.

The contents of the pictures were not the same, though. The cat's tail had become an arm, the bacon had become red lines, and the hills outside the window had become green circles.

How it worked

The first AI is given the subject in words and draws it. That drawing is turned into an image and shown to the next AI. The only request is to copy the same picture. It is not told the subject, and it is not told this is a telephone game.

The new drawing is turned into an image again and shown to the AI after that. This continues to round 100.

  • Every round is a fresh conversation. It sees only the one previous picture and nothing earlier
  • The request text is the same every round
  • The AI does not render its own drawing to check it
  • No redraws or fixes because of how a picture turned out
  • Subjects are everyday words only. No existing characters or works

The three subjects were "a cat riding a bicycle", "a seaside with a lighthouse" and "a breakfast table". They were chosen to differ: a creature with a machine, a landscape, and a scene with many small objects.

The pictures were drawn as SVG, a format that builds a picture out of shapes. It does not blur the way a photo of a photo would. If anything changes, it is because of how an AI read the previous picture.

A cat riding a bicycle

In the first three rounds the picture was simplified a lot. The markings on the face, the thin lines of the wheels and the chain disappeared. The number of shapes went from 94 in round 1 to 61 in round 2.

After that, it changed slowly.

The clearest case is the tail. In round 1 it was a curled, raised tail. It shifted shape little by little, and from around round 14 it looked like a raised arm. The AI that drew round 17 wrote "a cat waving its hand" in its own report. The AI that drew round 1 had written that the cat was holding the handlebars with both hands.

Once the tail had become an arm, it did not go back to being a tail. For the next AI, it had been an arm from the start. The cat in round 100 holds one arm straight out to the side.

Other things changed too. The blue scarf became a band across the chest. The pedal became a red box and then a bright pink square. The sky went from light blue to deep blue-violet, and the sun from yellow to orange.

A seaside with a lighthouse

Round 1 was a dusk scene in muted colors. By round 10 the sky was pink and purple and the sea was emerald. The colors kept getting stronger with each round after that.

The birds in the sky became symbols like wavy lines and arcs. One of the clouds became a dark purple ellipse. The hut beside the lighthouse became something like a pink wall.

The rock had a pale triangle that stood for its lit face. Between rounds 70 and 80, that triangle turned blue. A shape that had described how the light fell came to be drawn as an object: a blue triangle.

A breakfast table

This subject has many small objects, and each of them turned into a different shape.

The wavy bacon became two red lines. The glass of orange juice became a striped cylinder and then something like a bundle of thin sticks. The toast became a yellow diamond, and the wood-grain table became plain. The steam rising from the coffee was gone by round 30.

The biggest change was outside the window. The hills started inside the window and grew with each round. Between rounds 60 and 70 they spilled past the window frame, and by round 100 they are two large green circles overlapping the window.

Asking a separate AI what each picture shows

Round 1 and every tenth round were shown, one picture at a time, to a separate AI that knew nothing. The only question was what the picture shows, in one sentence. The answers were given in Japanese and are translated here.

Subject still recognized at round 1003 / 3

These were the answers for the round-100 pictures.

  • A picture of an orange cat riding a red bicycle along a road under a clear blue sky.
  • A flat illustration of a seaside at sunset, with a glowing lighthouse striped in pink and white, a small boat, and a starfish on the sand.
  • An illustration of a breakfast scene: a table with a plate of fried egg, toast, a fork, a knife, a blue mug and a jar of jam, with the sun and green trees visible through the window.

All three subjects are still there. Lined up by round, though, the answers show things that dropped out or changed along the way.

SubjectWhat dropped out of the answers or changed
A cat riding a bicycle"Blue scarf" appears in the answers up to round 20 and in none from round 30 on
A seaside with a lighthouse"Red and white stripes" became "pink and white stripes" from round 90

What the three had in common

  • A large simplification in roughly the first ten rounds, then slow change
  • Colors that grew stronger and more vivid with each round
  • A misreading, once made, was never undone. From the next round on it was handed down as the original

The plan assumed that after enough rounds a picture might fall apart into something else, and it included a rule to stop a chain once there was nothing left to copy. That rule was never used. All three reached round 100 while still working as pictures.

WHAT REMAINED

The frame stayed and the details were swapped

In this experiment, Claude Code running Claude Opus 5.5 copied each of three subjects 100 times. What each picture was of still came through to a separate AI at round 100.

What changed was the details. They did not fade into a blur. A tail became an arm, hills became green circles, and the lit face of a rock became a blue triangle. Each was replaced by something else that was just as definite.

There are clear limits on what this supports. There were three subjects and each was run once. Running the same subject again would produce a different drift. One kind of AI was used, and the AI that looked at the pictures and answered was the same kind. A person looking at them might see it differently.

The descriptions of how the pictures changed were put together by the AI from looking at every tenth round side by side. The rounds in between were not checked one by one. The pictures themselves are shown above, so please compare them.

What 100 rounds of the telephone game lost was not the picture but the small facts inside it.

Experiment data

Date

Rounds
3 subjects × 100 = 300. Rounds 1 to 20 of the cat came from the pilot, and the main run continued from round 21
How each round ran
A fresh conversation (subagent) per round, given the one previous image and the same request text
Turning drawings into images
The same method every round, keeping the drawing's own proportions with the longer side at 512 pixels. All 300 images are 512×384
Number of shapes (round 1 → 50 → 100)
Cat 94→59→39 / Lighthouse 65→53→52 / Breakfast 66→58→45
Chains that stopped early
None. No drawing contained unsafe content
Rounds that were redrawn
Two. Round 2 of the pilot, because a flaw in the image conversion had added white bands above and below the picture, and round 73 of the breakfast table, because the usage limit cut the session off mid-drawing. No round was redrawn because of how it looked
Interruption
The run stopped once at the usage limit and resumed with the same request text (at round 79 for the cat, 68 for the lighthouse and 72 for the breakfast table)
Behind-the-scenes work
Converting each drawing to an image and passing it to the next round was done by a separate, smaller AI model that only ran one fixed command. For round 80 of the cat, the same command was run by hand
Milestone check
33 pictures (round 1 and every tenth round), each shown to a separate fresh conversation that answered in one sentence
Roles
Claude Code: proposals, draft conditions, running the experiment, records and the draft of this article. Human: choosing the plan, approving the conditions and approving publication
